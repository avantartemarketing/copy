# What the framework covers, and what it never will

A review of the whole Notion comms archive (11,753 items, September 2023 to 2027) and the HubSpot
export (3,630 emails), looking for releases the framework cannot generate. For each, the question
was whether it could have been written differently so that it fits, or whether it will always be
written by hand.

The framework is: one skeleton per output set, four fragments per release (bio, hook, making,
quote), a features list, and facts. Two mechanics, draw and timed. See the README.

## The headline

Of the 262 release campaigns since January 2025 that record a launch mechanic, 164 record one that
is readable. Of those, 150 are draw or timed, which is 91%. The remaining 14 use four other
mechanics, and all four are cheap to add. What the framework will never cover is a different
question: about a third of our own Instagram feed is editorial rather than release comms, and no
part of it belongs in the system.

## 1. Mechanics the framework lacks, all reworkable

Each of these is one more value in the mechanic cell plus one or two action lines. The skeleton,
the fragments and the features line are unchanged. The Ahmed Mater announcement is the proof:
strip the action line and it is already exactly what the framework produces.

| Mechanic | Campaigns | The action line it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Ranked auction | Claire Silver (daydreamer), Ahmed Mater (Magnetism 25) | "Link in bio to bid in the ranked auction for a chance to collect. Bidding closes Tuesday 21 October at 10:00 UK time." Plus a locked rules line: "The top 40 bidders will win an edition, with print numbers assigned by final bid rank." |
| Enquiry | Robert Nava (Death Carrier Bunny), Grayson Perry (Magical Thinking, Large) | "Available now in a limited edition of just 20. Head to the link in our bio to enquire." |
| Open, first come first served | Pejac (HOPE and SELF-DEFENCE), Benrei Huang, Yutaka Hashimoto, En Iwamura | No deadline at all, so the one deadline form is simply omitted. |
| Pre-order | Giorgiko, Takahiro Komuro | "Link in bio to pre-order." |

Private sales (Grant Yun, Ayako Rokkaku, a James Jean work) are not public campaigns, so they need
nothing.

**The one caveat on auctions.** The two auction campaigns are written in different voices. Ahmed
Mater is house voice and fits. Claire Silver is crypto-native and does not: on-chain ETH bidding,
numbered rule threads, "Don't be late! ⌛", "🚨 The ranked auction opens this Friday!". The validator
would reject nearly every line of it, correctly, because it is not the house voice. That is an
audience decision, not a framework gap.

## 2. Release shapes the framework cannot describe

These are real gaps. Some can be reworked, and the rework costs something.

- **Every print different.** Five campaigns release works where no two are the same: Pejac's HOPE
  (1,000 unique hand-finished prints), Pejac's SELF-DEFENCE (12 unique prints), William Mapan's
  Centrifuge (35 generative prints), Iskra Velitchkova, and mpkoz. The edition phrase is free text,
  so "1,000 unique hand-finished prints, no two the same" fits, and "Hand-finished in 24 carat gold
  leaf" is a feature. What breaks is the card, which assumes one card per title. **Reworkable, except
  the cards.**
- **Several drops.** HOPE launched in eight equal drops over four days. The framework has one launch
  and one close. Reworkable only by treating each drop as its own release, which loses the arc that
  made the campaign work. **Partly reworkable, at a cost.**
- **Two versions of one release.** SELF-DEFENCE offered twelve separate editions of 25 and a second
  configuration. One edition phrase cannot hold both. **Needs a second edition phrase, or stays
  written.**
- **A release built around an event.** Miwa Komatsu painted live under the ceiling of Fitzrovia
  Chapel, streamed, with the edition based on the work she made. The announcement fits the
  framework. The event posts, with their own place and time and a bilingual caption, do not.
  **Announcement reworkable, event posts bespoke.**
- **A print paired with an NFT.** Claire Silver's edition came with a 1/1/40 NFT. That is one more
  feature line. **Reworkable.**

## 3. Always bespoke, and rightly so

None of this is release comms, and none of it should enter the system.

| Kind | Examples |
|---|---|
| Games and hunts | Murakami's global hunt with location clues, the Jeff Koons Split-Rocker video game, Cattelan's "tape a banana to a window", James Jean's Maze |
| Editorial artist features | Danielle McKinney, Olafur Eliasson, Christo and Jeanne-Claude, Do Ho Suh, David Shrigley, Andy Goldsworthy, Erwin Wurm, Ana Mendieta, and about forty more |
| Exhibition news | Grayson Perry at the Wallace Collection, Ai Weiwei at Lisson, exhibition round-ups |
| Film and streaming | Ai Weiwei's documentaries, "7 days to stream" |
| Past works carousels | One per campaign, image-led, already understood as bespoke |
| Company and essays | Report reveals, image banks, Arte.bio |

## 4. Two conventions the framework drops

- **Photo and copyright credits.** 54 of 1,202 feed posts carry one, concentrated in 2024 and 2026:
  "©Anish Kapoor", "📷 @orestionline", "Images courtesy of @bridgemanimages". This is a trailing
  credits field on the post, and worth adding.
- **Localisation.** Japanese and Korean versions exist for Miwa Komatsu, Lee Ufan, Kukwon Woo and
  Murakami, sometimes as a second half of the same caption. The framework is English only.

## 5. The finding that matters most: the voice moved

Going back to 2023 and 2024, the announcement posts do not fit the framework at all, and the reason
is not mechanics. It is that the house voice was different. Openers then were editorial:

> Self-satire meets storied symbolism in a silkscreen print by @aiww.

> How do you make a moment last forever? Cai Guo-Qiang is an expert.

> First spotted in 2008, Nini has taken on a life of his own in Benrei's surreal, pastel-hued vistas.

> In the same way that the patterns in the terrain follow a formula but are ultimately entirely
> unique, the work of artist Matt DesLauriers relies on chance.

Each could be rewritten as a status line plus a hook, and the facts survive. The personality does
not. The second of those opens with a question, which the validator now blocks outright.

So the framework is a description of where the voice landed in 2025 and 2026, not a description of
Avant Arte's voice in general. Applied in 2024 it would have flattened a more varied feed. Applied
now it codifies a convergence that had already happened, which is why it fits the recent archive so
well. It should be revisited whenever the voice moves again, and the fragments are the place where
personality is meant to live.
